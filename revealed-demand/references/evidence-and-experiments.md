# Evidence and experiment standard

Use this reference when the task requires signal interpretation, pricing, funnel analysis, or an executable market test.

## 1. Evidence is graded, not binary

Observable action is generated by preferences, constraints, information, trust, timing, and the available alternatives. Therefore, describe what a signal supports rather than treating any action as pure truth.

### Signal families

| Family | Examples | Usually supports | Common confounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention | impressions, views, dwell time | the message reached people | platform distribution, curiosity, autoplay |
| Engagement | likes, saves, comments, shares | relevance or social utility | low effort, incentives, audience mismatch |
| Intent | landing-page clicks, waitlist, reply, booked call | willingness to spend attention or information | no price exposure, novelty, weak qualification |
| Commitment | detailed application, deposit, paid pilot, pre-order | willingness to accept cost or risk | refundability, social relationship, discounts |
| Transaction | paid order, completed checkout | willingness to pay for this offer under these conditions | gifting, bundling, heavy subsidy, refunds |
| Retention | activation, repeat use, renewal, referral, repeat purchase | delivered value and possible repeatability | lock-in, contract terms, limited alternatives |

Do not collapse these families into one score without explaining the loss of meaning.

## 2. Five signal-quality dimensions

Assess each meaningful signal on:

1. User cost — money, time, information, reputation, or switching effort.
2. Specificity — whether the action targets the exact promised result.
3. Reversibility — whether the action can be undone at little cost.
4. Independence — whether the action came from an unaffiliated buyer without pressure.
5. Recurrence — whether it repeats across time, cohorts, or channels.

Use qualitative labels such as low, medium, and high only when the underlying facts are shown.

## 3. Evidence ledger

For every decisive claim, record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Signal | The observed or reported action |
| Numerator | Number completing the action |
| Denominator | Number meaningfully exposed or eligible |
| Window | Dates or duration |
| Cohort | Segment, channel, and source |
| Ask | Price, deposit, time, data, or other cost |
| Provenance | Observed, user-reported, imported, or inferred |
| Confounds | Incentive, warm audience, discount, bundle, missing tracking |
| Supports | The narrow conclusion justified |
| Does not support | The tempting but unjustified conclusion |

If a denominator is absent, report a count, not a conversion rate.

## 4. Funnel interpretation

Prefer a funnel that mirrors the real decision:

meaningful exposure → call-to-action response → checkout or commitment start → paid → activated → retained or repeated

Do not assume the largest numeric drop is the causal bottleneck. Check tracking integrity, cohort mix, and whether the step represents the intended user action.

Useful metrics include:

- call-to-action rate = responses / meaningful exposures;
- checkout rate = checkouts / responses;
- gross purchase conversion = purchases / meaningful exposures;
- net retained conversion = non-refunded purchases / meaningful exposures;
- refund rate = refunds / purchases;
- activation rate = activated purchasers / non-refunded purchases;
- contribution per exposure = (revenue - variable delivery cost) / meaningful exposures.

Pair every rate with counts and a time window. For small samples, show an interval and avoid ranking close cells as winners.

For the bundled helper, revenue means recognized revenue after discounts and refunds. The repeat_buyers field counts original buyers who made at least one later purchase; it is not the total number of repeat orders.

## 5. Transaction unit test

A good minimum transaction unit is:

- narrow enough to explain in one sentence;
- valuable before a large commitment;
- deliverable with current capability;
- fast enough to create an observable value moment;
- instrumented so success and failure are visible;
- bounded so the buyer knows what is excluded.

If the buyer must understand a platform, curriculum, or roadmap before understanding the result, the unit is still too large.

## 6. Price testing

Price is part of the offer, not a standalone truth. Form hypotheses using:

- cost of the current workaround;
- cost of delay or failure;
- price of credible alternatives;
- decision friction for the buying context;
- delivery and support cost;
- capacity and quality constraints.

Use two or three price cells only when traffic and assignment quality can support comparison. Otherwise use sequential cohorts, a paid pilot, or a deposit test and label the evidence accordingly.

Before launch, define:

- the price difference and why it is decision-relevant;
- who is eligible for each cell;
- how assignment occurs;
- refund terms;
- primary outcome;
- minimum observation window;
- pass, ambiguous, and fail actions.

Do not declare an optimal price from a single cell or a small difference inside wide uncertainty.

## 7. Decision discipline

Choose thresholds before seeing the result. A threshold must be linked to a decision, not chosen because it sounds ambitious.

Example:

- Pass: at least 8 paid buyers from 120 qualified exposures, refund rate below 10%, and delivery completed within the capacity limit. Run one controlled expansion.
- Ambiguous: 4–7 buyers or incomplete tracking. Repeat with repaired instrumentation.
- Fail: 0–3 buyers after the full window with verified exposure. Interview recent non-buyers, then repackage or stop.

The numbers above are illustrative. Replace them with case-specific economics and capacity.

## 8. Claim language

Prefer:

- This cohort provides transaction evidence for this offer at this price.
- The result is consistent with price friction, but packaging and channel remain plausible rivals.
- The evidence does not yet establish repeatable demand outside the warm audience.

Avoid:

- The market definitely wants this.
- This is the perfect price.
- The experiment proves the message caused the sales.
