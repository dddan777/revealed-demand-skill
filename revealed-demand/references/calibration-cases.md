# Calibration cases

These cases calibrate decisions. They are not scripts to copy.

## Case 1 — Idea with survey enthusiasm

Input:

- A creator says 73 of 100 survey respondents would use an AI study planner.
- No respondent saw a price or completed a high-cost action.

Correct boundary:

- Evidence state: problem evidence at most.
- Do not call the idea validated or choose an optimal subscription price.
- Next test: offer a narrowly defined paid planning sprint or refundable deposit to a qualified cohort.

Failure pattern:

- Converting stated interest into a revenue forecast.

## Case 2 — MVP with attention but little use

Input:

- 8,000 content views, 300 site visits, 42 registrations, 3 completed core actions, and no purchases.

Correct boundary:

- Attention and some intent exist; transaction demand is unproven.
- Registration-to-core-action completion is about 7.1%, so scaling traffic would amplify unresolved activation or offer friction.
- Next test: a smaller paid result with a shorter path to value, observed with a single qualified cohort.

Failure pattern:

- Recommending more channels before the core value moment is observable.

## Case 3 — More than 1,000 paid lesson-pack orders

Input:

- A practical lesson pack helps humanities and social-science learners use large language models for text analysis.
- The creator reports more than 1,000 paid orders.
- Price, exposure denominator, refund rate, cohort mix, and repeat behavior are not yet supplied.

Correct boundary:

- The reported orders are strong transaction evidence for the historical offer.
- The result does not by itself establish price elasticity, causality, retention, or demand in a new channel.
- Next action: reconstruct the historical evidence ledger, then test one expansion claim such as a new price, adjacent outcome, or cold-audience channel.

Failure pattern:

- Treating a large sales count as proof that every adjacent course, tool, or subscription will sell.
